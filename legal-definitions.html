Word of the Day

When you're inside the courthouse — or writing and reading any papers that came from or are being submitted to the court — there are words that can trip you up.

These are words we all use in our everyday language without ever thinking about what they mean. They're so common and ordinary that we never need to. But one of the many things you should know — and are never informed of — is that inside the courthouse, these same words have very different meanings.

They even have their very own dictionary.

Unfortunately for those of us on a budget, the current version — with over 2,500 newly added words to keep up with technology and the times — isn't available anywhere for free. You can access the version from six editions ago, but that doesn't help you now. There is no web version of their official dictionary. If you have $150 a month to pay for a legal resource website membership, you can view it there. Or your option is to buy the dictionary, available only in hardcover, for $200.

Most people can't afford that.

A friend purchased one for me for my birthday, and I'm going to use it. Each week, I'll pick 2 or 3 words from the comments on each episode's YouTube page, and the following week at the end of the episode, I'll read off the definitions and add them to this page.

Here are a few examples to get us started:

Week 1

CAPACITY

Everyday Definition:

How much someone or something can do or hold; size or volume.

Legal Definition:

The legal ability or status to sue, be sued, contract, inherit, or act for another.

PRAYER

Everyday Definition:

An address to God or a god, spoken or thought; an earnest request; the act of praying; a religious service made up chiefly of prayers.

Legal Definition:

A request for relief in a legal complaint. A special prayer asks for particular relief, while a general prayer asks for additional, unspecified relief.

CONVERSION

Everyday Definition:

A change from one form, use, or condition to another.

Legal Definition:

A civil wrong involving wrongful possession, control, or dominion over another person's personal property.

TENDER

Everyday Definition:

Gentle or soft; can also refer to an offer.

Legal Definition:

An unconditional offer to pay money or to perform what is owed.

INSTRUMENT

Everyday Definition:

A tool, device, or implement; also a musical device used to produce sound.

Legal Definition:

A formal legal document, such as a deed, will, promissory note, contract, or bond, that creates, records, or transfers rights or obligations.

SIT

Everyday Definition:

Resting in a seated position; staying in one place; being located somewhere.

Legal Definition:

Of a judge, to occupy a judicial seat, hold court, or perform official functions. Of a court or legislative body, to hold proceedings.

SHARP

Everyday Definition:

Having an edge; quick-witted; clearly defined.

Legal Definition:

A clause in a mortgage deed or similar instrument that allows the creditor to take immediate and summary action upon the debtor's default.

SHEER

Everyday Definition:

Very thin and see-through; complete or total; very steep.

Legal Definition:

In maritime law, a vessel's sudden deviation from its line of course — a swerve.

SHELVING

Everyday Definition:

The shelves themselves; material used to make shelves; sometimes a sloping surface.

Legal Definition:

In patent law, failing to begin commercial use, or stopping commercial use, of a patented product during the relevant period.

SERVE

Everyday Definition:

Helping, providing for, or performing a duty for someone; serving food; putting the ball in play in sports.

Legal Definition:

To make legal delivery of a notice or legal process, or to present a person with that notice or process as required by law.

SENTENCE

Everyday Definition:

A group of words that expresses a complete thought, usually with a subject and a verb.

Legal Definition:

In criminal procedure, the judgment a court formally pronounces after a finding of guilt, including the punishment imposed on the offender.

RELIEF

Everyday Definition:

The easing of pain, stress, or trouble; help given to people in need.

Legal Definition:

An heir's payment to a feudal lord for the privilege of succeeding to an ancestor's tenancy; also aid or assistance provided by the state.

Week 3

CONSIDERATION

Everyday Definition:

Careful thought; taking something into account; thoughtful attention to something before making a decision.

Legal Definition (Short Version):

In contract law, something one side gives, does, gives up, or promises in exchange for the other side's promise. It's what usually makes a deal legally enforceable.

WHERE THE WORD COMES FROM

"Consideration" comes from Latin. It originally meant to observe the stars. Over time it came to mean looking at something closely, thinking it over, and finally, in law, the thing that makes a promise worth enforcing. Legal writers point out that for centuries the word had no special legal meaning at all. It took until roughly the 1800s for lawyers to build a real legal theory around the word.

THE FOUR MAIN LEGAL MEANINGS

1. Contracts:

Something one side gives, does, gives up, or promises in exchange for the other side's promise. It's what usually makes a deal legally enforceable.


2. Parliamentary Law:

The process a group (like a board, council or legislature) uses to handle a motion, from the moment the chair states it until the vote.


3. Everyday Meaning:

Something you take into account when forming an opinion.


4. Historical:

In old English law, a court's judgment. Old court records opened their rulings with Latin words meaning "it is considered by the court."

TYPES OF CONSIDERATION — BY VALUE

Adequate Consideration:

Fair and reasonable for the deal.


Inadequate Consideration:

Not fair or reasonable for the deal.


Grossly Inadequate Consideration:

So far below fair value that the deal may not count as a real sale at all. Depending on the facts, it may really be a gift, or even a fraud.


Sufficient Consideration (also: legally sufficient consideration):

Enough to support a contract under the law. Modern contract law mostly asks whether something was bargained for, not whether the price was fair.


Valuable Consideration (also: legal consideration, good and valuable consideration):

Gives one side a benefit, or puts a burden on the other, that can be measured in money.


Good Consideration (also: meritorious or moral consideration):

Based on love, affection, family ties, or moral duty. Usually NOT enough to enforce a contract. A parent's promise to give a child a gift, for example, generally can't be enforced just because of love.


Nominal Consideration:

So small it has no real relation to the value exchanged, like one dollar for a house.


Gratuitous Consideration:

Costs the giver nothing, or is something they were already required to do. Won't support a contract.

TYPES OF CONSIDERATION — BY TIMING

Concurrent Consideration:

Both sides' promises or performances happen at the same time.


Continuing Consideration:

A performance that goes on over a period of time.


Executed Consideration:

Already fully given.


Executory Consideration:

To be given after the contract is made.


Future Consideration:

To be given later, or not yet worked out between the parties.


Past Consideration:

Something done before the promise was made. Usually it doesn't count, because it wasn't given in exchange for the promise. Exceptions exist, such as a new promise to pay a debt that's too old to sue on, or one wiped out in bankruptcy.

TYPES OF CONSIDERATION — BY NATURE

Express Consideration:

Specifically written in the document.


Implied Consideration:

Not written down, but the law infers it from how the parties acted.


Fair Consideration:

Roughly equal in value to what's received, given in good faith.


Other Consideration (also: "other good and valuable consideration"):

Extra things of value that aren't listed in the document, either because there are too many to list or to keep the full price private.


Illegal Consideration:

Against the law or public policy. Won't support a contract.


Immoral Consideration:

So offensive to society's standards that it's invalid. A contract built on it usually can't be enforced.


Illusory Consideration:

A promise to do something the person was already required to do, like a public duty or an existing contract. It isn't real consideration because nothing new is promised.


Impossible Consideration:

A promise or performance that can't actually be done.


Sham Consideration:

Looks valuable on paper but was never actually delivered. Sometimes used to make a gift look like a sale.


Invented Consideration:

Consideration a court treats as existing so a contract doesn't fail.

RELATED TERMS YOU'LL SEE

Failure of Consideration:

The consideration was promised, but it was never delivered, or it turned out to be worthless.


Want of Consideration (also: lack of consideration):

There was never any consideration to begin with, so there was no enforceable contract.
