Questions Answered in Episode 3

How much does it cost to hire a lawyer?

It varies widely. The lowest quote I ever received from an attorney was $3,000. For a simple speeding or red-light ticket, some lawyers charge a flat fee of about $100 to $500, while others charge that much per hour. Meanwhile, the Federal Reserve's survey of U.S. households found that more than a third of adults could not cover a surprise $400 expense with cash. That is why more people are walking into court representing themselves.

How do you check whether something is really the law?

Stop asking "what does the law say?" and start asking "where does it actually say that?" Then read the source yourself: the statute, the court rule, the court opinion, the docket. Not someone's summary of it. Confidence is not a citation. Verification is.

Can you ask a methadone program to taper you off?

Yes. Federal rules for opioid treatment programs require them to give a patient who chooses to taper the chance to do it, at a rate the patient and the program agree on (42 C.F.R. § 8.12).

Who is supposed to get opioid medication in a Maryland jail?

Maryland law (Correctional Services § 9-603) requires local jails to assess inmates for opioid use disorder and to provide medication to inmates for whom that treatment is determined to be appropriate, after an evaluation by a health care practitioner who can prescribe. A diagnosis comes first.

How do you request public records in Maryland?

Use the Maryland Public Information Act. You do not need a lawyer. Write to the agency and ask for the record. They generally have 30 days to respond, the first two hours of searching are free, and you can ask them to waive fees if you cannot afford them. Be specific: name the exact record and the date range. Vague requests get stalled. Specific requests get answered.

How do you get a copy of your own medical records in Maryland?

Ask the health care provider in writing. Maryland law requires them to give you a copy within a reasonable time, and a provider who refuses for more than 21 working days can be liable for damages (Health-General § 4-309). Get your evaluations and treatment records in your hands before you walk into court.

Is what you type into ChatGPT or another AI about your case private?

Not necessarily. In 2026, federal courts split. In United States v. Heppner (S.D.N.Y.), a defendant's AI-generated documents were not protected. In Warner v. Gilbarco (E.D. Mich.), a self-represented woman's AI-assisted work was protected as work product, and the judge wrote that AI programs are "tools, not persons." In Morgan v. V2X (D. Colo.), the court said work product protection could apply, but the specific system, its terms and what you put into it all matter. None of these decisions binds a Maryland court. If you represent yourself, there is no attorney-client privilege with a chatbot.

How do you check whether a court case AI gave you is real?

Pull up the case and read it. Find the exact quote in the opinion. If you cannot find it, do not use it. In Mezu v. Mezu (Appellate Court of Maryland, 2025), a lawyer filed a brief with fictitious and misquoted cases found with AI, and the court referred him to the Attorney Grievance Commission. Using AI is not the problem. Failing to check it is.

What does "consideration" mean in law?

In everyday life it means thinking something over. In the law, Black's Law Dictionary describes consideration as something bargained for and received in exchange for a promise, and it is generally necessary for an agreement to be enforceable. The word comes from Latin and originally meant to observe the stars.

This is legal information and my own experience, not legal advice. Laws and rules change and every state is different, so check the current rule yourself.
