What Your Ticket Is Actually Supposed to Cost

Every state publishes what a traffic violation costs before you ever see the inside of a courtroom. Maryland calls it the Schedule of Preset Motor Vehicle Fines, form DC-CR-090. Other states call it a bail schedule, a payables list, or a waiver schedule. A few do not publish one at all, because they let every court set its own.

Find your state below. Then look up the exact statute number printed on your citation — not the words, the number. If the charge on your paper does not match the penalty on the schedule, that is worth knowing before you agree to anything.

Why Some States Say "No Statewide Schedule"

Arizona, Indiana, Kentucky, Michigan, Ohio, Oklahoma and Texas do not publish one document for the whole state. Each court sets its own. That is not me failing to find it. That is the answer, and it is worth knowing, because it means the number you are quoted can change from one courthouse to the next inside the same state.

If your state is on that list, go to your state's court website, find your specific court, and look for a fine schedule, waiver schedule, bond schedule, or deposit schedule.
