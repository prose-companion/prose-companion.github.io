Questions Answered in Episode 1

What does "pro se" mean?

It is Latin for "for oneself" or "on one's own behalf." Going pro se means you represent yourself in court without a lawyer: you sign your own filings, write your own motions, and are expected to follow the same rules and deadlines a lawyer would. In California you will usually hear "pro per" instead. Same idea.

Is a motor scooter a "motor vehicle" under Maryland law?

No. Maryland's definition of "motor vehicle" in the Transportation Article, § 11-135, does not include a moped or a motor scooter. Scooters have their own separate rules, including their own insurance statute, § 17-104.1.

What is Maryland form DC-CR-090?

It is the District Court of Maryland's Schedule of Preset Motor Vehicle Fines. It lists traffic violations by statute number with the preset fine, and states that officers are not authorized to deviate from it. Every state has some version of this. Find yours in the table on this page.

Why does the statute number on a ticket matter so much?

Because the number decides the penalty. In this episode, the scooter insurance violation (§ 17-104.1) carried a $290 preset fine with no points and no jail. The ticket was written under the uninsured motor vehicle statute (§ 17-107) instead, which is a criminal offense that can carry jail time. That one number is what triggered the probation violation. Always compare the statute number on your paper to your state's fine schedule.

How long do you have to appeal in Maryland?

Generally 30 days. A notice of appeal is due within 30 days after the judgment (Md. Rule 8-202(a)), and an application for leave to appeal is also due within 30 days (Md. Rule 8-204(b)(2)). There is a separate, shorter deadline to order the transcript (Md. Rule 8-411(b)). Check the current rules yourself, because deadlines can change.

Do you have a right to represent yourself?

In federal court, 28 U.S.C. § 1654 says parties may plead and conduct their own cases personally. Each state has its own rules for self-representation. The links for your state are on the main map.

This is legal information and my own experience, not legal advice. Laws and rules change and every state is different, so check the current rule yourself.
